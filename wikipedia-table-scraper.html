Scrape a Wikipedia table into a spreadsheet.

Open the article, click the icon, and the table is already a spreadsheet: its own headers as column names, and every link kept as a column of its own. Pages with several tables offer the others under Wrong list?.

Install for Chrome — free Install for Edge Install for Firefox Free exports, no paywall on your data. No account.

Tested 4 November 2026 on List of largest cities: 85 rows and 17 columns on the first page. Download the GIF

What comes out

  • The table’s own headers as column names, joined where a header spans two rows (“City proper Population”)
  • Each linked cell’s address as a column beside it: City Link, Country Link
  • Flag and thumbnail images as image addresses
  • On another article, List of countries by population, 240 rows came out with Location, Population, % of world, Date and Source

How to do it

01

Open the article

Any Wikipedia page with a table, in any language.

02

Click the icon

The biggest table is picked first. Wrong list? → Another table moves to the next one; this page had 19 others.

03

Tidy it

Click a header to leave a column out, double-click to rename it.

04

Export

CSV, Excel or JSON, or paste into a new Google Sheet.

Going further, and the limits

Footnote markers such as [1] stay in the text, as Wikipedia writes them. For a whole category of articles, drill-down can open each linked article and add what it finds there; free runs it on three rows.

Whether you may collect from a site is between you and that site: its terms, its robots.txt and data-protection law. Magic Scraper reads only what your browser already shows you, paces itself gently by default, and a “verify you’re human” check stops the run and hands you the tab; it never tries to get past one.

Reasonable questions

Is there a free Wikipedia scraper?

Yes. Magic Scraper reads Wikipedia pages and exports CSV, Excel or JSON for free, with no account and no row cap. In our test on List of largest cities it found 85 rows and 17 columns on the first page.

Did you really test it?

Yes, on 4 November 2026, with the released extension, on the page shown in the clip. Sites change; if yours does not come out right, tell us and it gets looked at.

Do I need an account or an API key?

No. It reads the page you are looking at, in your own browser. Nothing is sent to us; there is no server of ours to send it to.

What if Wikipedia shows me a check?

A “verify you’re human” check stops the run and hands you the tab. Magic Scraper never tries to get past one.
