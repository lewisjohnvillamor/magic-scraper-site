Two tables, one page, different column counts. Automatic detection picks the bigger one — the 14-row international table — so if you want the 8-row domestic one you have to ask for it. That is what the Another table control is for.

About this practice page

Shipping rates in two tables

Couriers and shops publish delivery charges like this: one table for home, one for abroad, never the same shape. If you are comparing carriers or working out shipping costs, you usually need both.

Why it is tricky

A tool that takes “the table on the page” gives you one and leaves you wondering where the other went. Magic Scraper opens on the bigger one — the international rates — and keeps the other one a click away.

How to do it

Open Magic Scraper: the 14 international rates are there. For the 8 domestic zones, open Wrong list? and press Another table; the outline moves so you can see which one you have before exporting. Keep the two in separate tabs of your sheet — their columns mean different things.

What you should get

14 rows by 6 columns (Country, Code, Transit, Flat rate, Free over, Duties), or 8 rows by 5 (Zone, Covers, Transit, Standard, Trade). Some cells are words: Free means 0, while a dash under Free over means there is no free threshold at all. DDP means duties are paid by the sender, DAP that they are collected on delivery.
