Sandbox · pagination

Seven kinds of Next button, to practise on.

Most scrapers stop at page one because they look for a link that says “Next”. Real sites say it with an arrow, a page number, “Older posts”, another language, a class name, or a button with no address at all. Here is the same list of thirty products paged each of those ways, so you can see which ones your tool follows.

The seven

Each list is 30 items over three pages of ten. Start on the first page and crawl; anything short of 30 means the Next was missed.

01

An arrow with no word

The Next control is an icon: a chevron in a circle with an aria-label and no visible text. Tests whether your scraper reads labels, not just link text.

Expected: 30 products across 3 pages.

02

Page numbers only

A row of page numbers with the current one marked, and no Next or arrow at all. Tests whether your scraper can work out which number comes next.

Expected: 30 products across 3 pages.

03

A blog’s “Older posts”

A journal of posts, newest first, paged by Older posts → and ← Newer posts. Tests whether “next” is understood as “older” here.

Expected: 30 posts across 3 pages.

04

Next in German

The same list in German: Weiter › and ‹ Zurück, prices written 39,00 €. Tests a scraper that only knows English.

Expected: 30 products across 3 pages.

05

Next in Japanese

The same list in Japanese, paged by 次へ and 前へ, with prices in yen. Tests a scraper that only knows the Latin alphabet.

Expected: 30 products across 3 pages.

06

A class name and nothing else

An empty, styled button whose only clue is class="pagination__next" — no text, no label, the arrow drawn in CSS. Tests whether your scraper reads the markup’s own names.

Expected: 30 products across 3 pages.

07

A button, and no address

One page. Next page is a <button> that swaps the products in with JavaScript; the address never changes. Tests whether your scraper can click as well as follow links.

Expected: 30 products from 3 clicks.

About these practice lists

Why “it only got the first page” is the commonest complaint

To go through a list, a scraper has to find the way to the next page, and most look for a link that says Next. Real sites rarely make it that easy: an arrow icon, “Older posts”, Weiter or 次へ, page numbers only, a button whose only clue is its style, or a button that loads the next page without changing the address. Each of the seven lists above is paged one of those ways, with the same thirty products.

How Magic Scraper handles them

It finds all seven on its own. It reads an arrow’s label, works out which number comes after the current page, understands that Older means forward on a blog, knows the word for “next” in more than 80 languages, and clicks buttons that have no address, waiting for the list to change each time.

How to do it

Open any of the seven lists on its first page and press Start crawling. To see which control it chose before you start, press Auto-detect “Next” on the Crawl tab. If a site ever has one it misses, Pick “Next” manually lets you click it yourself.

What you should get

30 items over 3 pages from every list. Ten means the way forward was missed; twenty usually means a crawler went forward and then back. More on this in when a scraper only scrapes the first page.
