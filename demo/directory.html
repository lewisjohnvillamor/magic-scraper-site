About this practice page

A local directory, and the lead list behind it

Trade directories, chamber of commerce member lists and “find a tradesperson” sites all look like this: a short card per business in the list, and the useful details — email, website, address, opening hours — one click away on its own profile page. If you are building a list of businesses to contact, this is the job, and it has two halves.

Watch it done on this page: “Turn any business directory into a lead list in 1 click” (YouTube)

The first half: every listing

The list shows a name, a trade and a phone number, eight to a page, over three pages. Collect all three pages and you have 24 businesses with a phone each — in Magic Scraper, open it on this page and press Start crawling.

The second half: what is on each profile

Each profile lays its details out as label and value pairs (Address, Phone, Email, Website, Opening hours, Established). Turn on drill-down, preview the first profile, and those become columns on every row. The free version reads the first three profiles so you can see the result; Pro reads all of them. Writing it yourself, it is a loop over the profile links, reading the pairs into a dictionary:

for link in soup.select("article.biz h3 a"):
    page = BeautifulSoup(requests.get(urljoin(url, link["href"])).text, "html.parser")
    labels = [dt.get_text(strip=True) for dt in page.select("dl.profile dt")]
    values = [dd.get_text(strip=True) for dd in page.select("dl.profile dd")]
    record = dict(zip(labels, values))

Checking the result, and using it well

24 rows; with the profiles read, six more columns. The phone number appears twice, once from the list and once from the profile — keep one. On a real directory, keep to business details, check the site’s terms, and mind the rules on contacting people where you are: is it legal to scrape a business directory?
