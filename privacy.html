Magic Scraper — Privacy Policy

Last updated 20 October 2026 · applies to the Magic Scraper browser extension for Chrome and Edge, and to Magic Scraper Desktop for Windows and Linux

Magic Scraper keeps your data on your machine. It has no servers of its own, no analytics and no telemetry. Everything it reads stays in your browser, and everything it produces goes only where you save it. Detecting a table, picking one by hand and exporting it make no network requests at all. The only requests the free extension ever makes are for further pages of the site you are already on, and only once you press Start crawling — never to a server of ours, because there isn't one. There is exactly one exception, described below, and it happens only if you buy Pro: your licence key is sent to Gumroad, once, to confirm the purchase.

What it reads

When you open the popup on a page, the extension reads that page's visible content — the text, links and image URLs already rendered in your browser — in order to find the repeating rows and turn them into a table. If you turn on drill-down, it also opens the pages your rows link to and reads them the same way.

It does not read pages you have not opened the popup on, does not run in the background, and does not touch form fields, passwords or cookies.

What it stores, and where

Everything is kept in your own browser. Nothing is uploaded. All of it is kept by the extension itself, in storage that belongs to the extension: no website can read it, and removing the extension deletes all of it.

If you used a version before 1.21

Earlier versions kept a crawl's rows and your past runs in storage belonging to the website you scraped, not to the extension. That had two consequences you should know about: the website's own scripts could see that the data was there and read it, and removing the extension did not delete it. Clear in the popup did not delete it either, although this policy said it did.

From 1.21 that data is moved into the extension and deleted from the website automatically, the next time you open Magic Scraper on that site. For a site you do not open it on again, clear that site's data in your browser (in Chrome: the padlock beside the address → Site settings → Delete data).

What leaves your machine

Your data: nothing. The only network requests the extension makes while scraping are to the site you are already on: when a site paginates by URL, it fetches the next page of that same site directly, exactly as your browser would if you clicked the link. Your rows, your columns, the pages you visit and the files you export never leave your machine.

One exception, and only if you buy Pro: activating your licence. When you paste a licence key and press Activate, the extension sends that key and the product ID to api.gumroad.com, once, so Gumroad can confirm the purchase. That is the whole request. It carries nothing about the page you are on, the data you have collected, or anything else about you. The answer is saved locally and never checked again — there is no call on startup, no periodic re-check and no telemetry. A free installation never makes this request at all, and never asks for permission to.

We do not store your name or email address, even though Gumroad's reply contains them.

Files you export (CSV, XLSX, JSON) are written to your own downloads folder. Where they go after that is up to you.

Two pages of this website, opened by your browser. When you first install the extension, it opens magicscraper.app/welcome, a page saying where the icon went. When you remove it, your browser opens magicscraper.app/goodbye, which asks why. The only thing the extension puts in either address is its version number, on the goodbye page, so an answer can be matched to the build it is about. Neither page receives anything you scraped, and the goodbye question is optional — see About this website.

Scheduled runs

If you schedule a saved setup, the extension opens that page in a background tab at the time you chose, collects it, writes a CSV to your downloads folder and closes the tab again — without you watching. Two things follow from that, and you should know both before you switch it on.

  • It visits that site in your browser, as you, on a timer. If you are signed in to the site, the run is signed in too, exactly as it would be if you opened the page yourself.
  • It writes files to your downloads folder while you are doing something else.

Nothing about those runs is sent anywhere. The rows, the files and the schedule itself stay on your machine, and a schedule stops the moment you switch it off or remove the extension.

Children

Magic Scraper is a developer and analyst tool. It is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.

Your responsibility when scraping

The extension automates reading pages you can already see. You remain responsible for respecting the terms of service, robots.txt, rate limits and copyright of the sites you use it on, and for handling any personal data you collect lawfully.

Magic Scraper Desktop (Windows and Linux)

Everything above is about the extension. The desktop app is a separate program with its own licence, and it does more on its own, so here is exactly what it keeps and what it sends.

What it keeps, on your computer. Your jobs, monitors and their run history, the rows each run collects (saved as they arrive, so a crash doesn't lose them), your settings, and your licence key with the purchase details Gumroad returns for it. It lives in your user data folder: %APPDATA%\MagicScraper on Windows (or a data folder beside the app in the portable edition) and ~/.local/share/magicscraper on Linux. Saved logins (the cookies of a site you signed in to through the app) and any proxy passwords you enter are stored there unencrypted, so anyone who can open your user account can read them; sign out of a saved login, or delete the folder, to remove them. Nothing here is sent to us.

About this website

Everything above is about the extension, which has no server and never did. This website is a different thing and now has one small piece of one, so it is worth saying plainly rather than leaving you to find it.

The feature request board, and the I’d use this buttons on the home page that feed it, store what was asked for and how many people want it. A request is the title and details someone typed and what it is for; new ones are read before they are shown. A vote is stored with a random id your browser makes up for itself, so that voting twice does not count twice. That id identifies nobody, is not a cookie, is not joined to anything, and is not shared. No name and no IP address is stored with a request or a vote.

The goodbye page works the same way. If you answer it, what is stored is the reason you picked, the version you had, the date, and anything you chose to type in its two optional boxes. No name, email or IP address is stored with it, and a typed answer is deleted after about a year.

The site counts page views with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is built into the hosting. It sets no cookies and does not identify or follow you: it reports totals, such as how many people opened a page and which site linked to it.

If you choose to leave an email address, on a request or after a vote, that address is kept, with the request you left it on, for one purpose: telling you when that thing ships. It is never shown on the board, it is deleted once we have told you, and asking to be removed at support@magicscraper.app is enough to delete it sooner.

If you email us to be told when Magic Scraper Desktop launches, your address is kept for that one message and nothing else; ask at support@magicscraper.app to be removed sooner.

None of this touches the extension. Beyond opening those two pages, it has no idea the website exists.
