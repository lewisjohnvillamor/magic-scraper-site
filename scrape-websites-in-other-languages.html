Scraping a site that isn't in English.

A scraper that finds the Next button by looking for the word “Next” stops at page one of a site that says 次へ, Weiter or Siguiente. A CSV opened in Excel can turn ü and 日本語 into gibberish, and a German 1.234 can arrive as one point two three four. Here is what goes wrong on sites in other languages, and what Magic Scraper does about each.

Page two, in any language

Most big sites mark their Next button in ways that do not depend on the language: a rel="next" link, an arrow, an aria-label, a numbered pager. Magic Scraper reads all of those, which is why Amazon.co.jp, Kleinanzeigen and LDLC crawl the same as an English shop. The word only matters on sites that write nothing else, and there it reads the word, in about 40 languages, and the Load more button too.

Accents and kanji that turn to gibberish in Excel

Excel guesses a CSV file's encoding, and without a hint it guesses wrong: Müller becomes MÃ¼ller and 東京 becomes æ±äº¬. Magic Scraper's CSV starts with the byte-order mark that tells Excel the file is UTF-8, so the letters arrive as the page wrote them. The Excel (.xlsx) export avoids the question altogether, and Google Sheets reads either.

A CSV from another tool that is already garbled can be rescued: in Excel, use Data → From Text/CSV and set File origin to 65001: Unicode (UTF-8).

1.234 is not one point two three four

Most of Europe writes 1.234,56 where English writes 1,234.56, and France writes 1 234,56. A spreadsheet that reads the dot as a decimal point turns a German table's 1.234 (one thousand two hundred and thirty-four) into 1.234, a thousand times too small, with nothing to say so.

From the next update next update, the Excel export looks at the whole column. When it shows European signs (83.200.000, 12,50, 1.234,56) every value is read that way, so the column sums. Thousands written apart with a space, the French way, are read in any column. A column of nothing but 1.234-style values could be either, so it stays text: never wrong, only not summable. The CSV keeps every value exactly as the page wrote it.

Column names stay in the page's language

A table's own headers come through as written: 価格, Preis, Precio. Where a page has no headers, columns are named from the markup, and those names are English: Price, Link, Image. Double-click a header in the popup, or press F2, to rename it; the name is remembered for that site.

The extension's own buttons and messages are in English. The data never is.

Reasonable questions

Why does my scraper stop at page one on a Japanese or German site?

Many scrapers find the next page by looking for the English word Next. Magic Scraper also reads rel="next" links, arrows, aria-labels and numbered pagers, and the word for Next in about 40 languages.

Why are the accents wrong when I open the CSV in Excel?

Excel guessed the wrong encoding. Magic Scraper's CSV carries the byte-order mark that tells Excel it is UTF-8, and its .xlsx export avoids the problem altogether.

Does it work on right-to-left sites in Arabic or Hebrew?

Yes. It reads the page's structure, not its direction, and from the next update it reads التالي and הבא as Next.

Is Magic Scraper available in my language?

The extension's buttons are in English. The data it collects stays in the language of the page.
