Scrape PubMed search results into a spreadsheet.

Run a search on PubMed, click the icon, and every paper arrives with its PMID, authors, journal, year and DOI in columns of their own, ready for a reference manager or a screening sheet.

Going further, and the limits

Need more than the result line? Drill-down opens each paper’s page and adds the fields it describes itself with, such as the full author list, publication date, DOI and, where the page provides it, the abstract. Free runs it on three rows. For thousands of records, NCBI’s E-utilities exist for exactly that.

Whether you may collect from a site is between you and that site: its terms, its robots.txt and data-protection law. Magic Scraper reads only what your browser already shows you, paces itself gently by default, and a “verify you’re human” check stops the run and hands you the tab; it never tries to get past one.

Reasonable questions

Is there a free PubMed scraper?

Yes. Magic Scraper reads PubMed search results with PMID, authors, journal, year and DOI, follows every page and exports CSV, Excel or JSON for free.

Can I get the abstracts?

Drill-down opens each paper’s page and adds what it finds there, including the abstract where the page provides it. Free runs it on three rows; Pro on every row.

How many results can it collect?

As many as you page through: it is built for the size of sample a person screens. For a dataset of tens of thousands, use NCBI’s E-utilities.

Do I need an account or an API key?

No. It reads the page you are looking at, in your own browser. Nothing is sent to us; there is no server of ours to send it to.

What happens if PubMed changes its design?

Magic Scraper falls back to reading the page the ordinary way, which usually still finds the list. Tell us and the site reading is updated in the next release.
