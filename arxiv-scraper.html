How to do it

01

Open a listing or a search

A subject’s new or recent listing, or arXiv’s search results.

02

03

04

CSV, Excel or JSON, or paste into a new Google Sheet. Free, no row cap.

Going further, and the limits

Drill-down can open each paper’s abstract page and add what it describes itself with, such as the publication date, DOI and PDF address. For bulk metadata, arXiv’s own API is the right tool.

Whether you may collect from a site is between you and that site: its terms, its robots.txt and data-protection law. Magic Scraper reads only what your browser already shows you, paces itself gently by default, and a “verify you’re human” check stops the run and hands you the tab; it never tries to get past one.

Reasonable questions

Is there a free arXiv scraper?

Yes. Magic Scraper reads arXiv listings and search results with ID, title, authors, subjects and PDF links, follows every page and exports CSV, Excel or JSON for free.

Does it include the abstracts?

Yes, wherever the page shows them, as search results do. On a listing without abstracts, drill-down can open each paper’s page for them.

Should I use this or the arXiv API?

For a few hundred papers you are looking through, this is quicker. For bulk metadata, use arXiv’s API.

Do I need an account or an API key?

No. It reads the page you are looking at, in your own browser. Nothing is sent to us; there is no server of ours to send it to.

What happens if arXiv changes its design?

Magic Scraper falls back to reading the page the ordinary way, which usually still finds the list. Tell us and the site reading is updated in the next release.
