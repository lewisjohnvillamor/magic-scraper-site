Comparison

A Thunderbit alternative, honestly.

Thunderbit describes what you want in words and lets an AI agent work out the fields, paid for in credits. Magic Scraper detects the table on the page in your browser, with no credits to count. Here is when each makes sense.

Install for Chrome — free Install for Edge Free: detection, every page, Excel/CSV/JSON export.

What's the same, and what isn't

Both turn a web page into rows you can export, without writing code, and both can visit the page behind each row.

The difference is how the fields are found and paid for. Thunderbit's AI reads the page from a description, and its paid plans run on monthly credits. Magic Scraper reads the page's own structure locally — no AI service, no credits — with free unlimited crawling and a one-time Pro. What that means in practice.

When to keep Thunderbit

When the page has no regular structure — prose, mixed layouts — and describing the fields in words is quicker than picking them, or when you want results sent straight to Google Sheets, Airtable or Notion. That is what an AI agent is good at.

Reasonable questions

Does Magic Scraper use AI?

No. It finds the repeating rows from the page's structure and names columns from the markup, which is fast, predictable and runs entirely in your browser.

Is it cheaper than Thunderbit?

For repeated, large jobs usually yes: crawling and export are free with no page limit, and Pro is paid once rather than monthly. For an occasional messy page, Thunderbit's free plan may be enough.

Can I use both?

Yes. They don't conflict.
